195-minute swing plan

Inside the value band

Most of the day's volume trades inside a narrow band. The plan is one bet, made over and over: price leaves that band, comes back to test its edge, and if the edge holds, you go with it.

RGLD · NASDAQ14–24 August 2026real bars, real signal

What the three letters mean

Every 195-minute bar has a volume profile — the horizontal histogram already sitting on your chart. It shows how many shares traded at each price during that bar. It is almost always fattest in the middle and thin at the extremes.

POC · point of control
The single price where the most shares changed hands. The fattest row. This is the market's centre of gravity for that bar.
VAH · value area high
The top edge of the band holding 70% of the bar's volume.
VAL · value area low
The bottom edge of that same band. This is the line you trade against on the long side.
Why it matters
Inside the band, buyers and sellers agreed. Outside it, they didn't. Price that falls out of the band and is pushed straight back in is telling you the low prices were rejected — someone was waiting there.

One real trade, start to finish

This is not a made-up example. It's what the rules produced on RGLD on 14 August 2026, from real 1-minute data. The shaded band is the value the previous six bars built: 228.75 to 238.25.

  1. The signal: a dip below the band that doesn't stick

    The bar traded down to 226.44 — below VAL at 228.75 — then closed back up at 230.50. Its own POC finished at 230.88, also above VAL. Sellers got the price below value and couldn't keep it there.

  2. The entry: five cents above the signal bar's high

    You don't buy the close. You place a buy-stop just above the signal bar's high and let the market come get you. If it never trades there, you were wrong and it cost you nothing.

    buy-stop = 233.56 (bar high) + 0.05 = 233.61  —  filled 17 Aug

  3. The stop: below the low that was rejected

    If price goes back under the bar that rejected it, the reason for the trade is gone. The stop sits under the lower of the bar's low and VAL, with a quarter of an ATR of slack so normal noise doesn't clip you.

    stop = min(226.44, 228.75) − 0.25 × 4.64 = 225.28  —  risk 8.33/share

  4. The size: the stop decides it, not your conviction

    Risk half a percent of the account on the distance between entry and stop. That's the only input.

    (100,000 × 0.005) ÷ 8.33 = 60 shares  —  $500 at risk

  5. The exits: three thirds, at structure

    Sell a third at each level, and move the stop to breakeven after the first. From then on the trade cannot lose.

    T1 238.25 band top  ·  T2 247.75 band top + band width  ·  T3 262.50 wider structure

    T1 and T2 both filled on the 19 Aug gap. T3 filled 24 Aug. The trade made 1.91× the amount risked over ten bars — about $955 on a $500 risk.

What had to be true first

The signal alone isn't enough. Four checks run before any order goes in, and any one of them failing means no trade.

passed

Is the trend up?

Price above its 20-bar average, that average rising, and the volume centre of gravity higher than it was five bars ago.

passed

Is the stop sane?

8.33 is 1.79× ATR — inside the 2× ceiling. A wider stop means the structure isn't clean enough to trade.

passed

Is it worth taking?

The main target must pay at least 1.5× what you risk. T2 at 247.75 pays 1.70×.

passed

Is the bar real?

Not a climax bar (range under 2.5× ATR), volume at least 60% of normal, and no earnings inside the next three sessions.

Before you trade any of this

These rules have not been proven to make money. Run over six months of RGLD they produced eleven signals, nine trades, and a small net loss. Nine trades on one stock cannot tell a good plan from a bad one — it's far too few to mean anything either way.

What the testing did settle is mechanical: which levels the triggers can sensibly key off, and where the earlier draft of the plan was simply unworkable. That part is worth keeping. The edge is still unproven, so paper-trade at least thirty of these across several stocks before risking real size.
